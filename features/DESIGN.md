# DESIGN — Catch

The orienting document for the agent that builds Catch, with the human as secondary reader. Highest altitude only: what Catch is, the shape it must keep, and the constraints the build must not cross. Every constraint carries `ruled` (the visionary's, citing the docket entry that fixed it) or `chosen` (the agent's own call). The feature files bind; this document explains. Nobody edits it outside a discussion: the agent holds the pen, every change appends a changelog line naming the ruling that sanctioned it, a `ruled` constraint changes only by the visionary's reversal, and a `chosen` one still takes a discussion. A change with no changelog line is drift.

## Identity

- Catch is an Even G2 app that stays dark while the wearer talks with people and, at the moment they would otherwise lose it, holds one thing on the lens: the name someone just told them, the promise they just made, or the sentence they were in the middle of when cut off. It exists so the wearer stays in the conversation instead of trying to remember. [ruled: D1, D2]
- It is the team's entry for the Even Realities challenge at LA Hacks AI 2026, built from the event's start: finished and demoable at every hour mark after hour four, with a ratified cut order for when the time runs out. [ruled: D118]
- Quiet by design: nothing on the lens but a dim dot unless something has just slipped; one dim line, lower third, a second and a half, then dark. Every catch is shown at the lowest readable brightness, because brightness is the only lever Catch has over what others might glimpse. [ruled: D14]

## Shape

- Four parts: the lens app (an Even Hub plugin running in the Even app's WebView on the phone, driving the glasses' containers and receiving their mic and touch events); and a screenless server that hears, judges and writes. There is no separate iPhone app and no website (D126); the phone's screens (history, conversation screen with tick-off and delete, jogger) live on the plugin's own page in the Even app (D130, D133, D134). [ruled: D102, D103, D126, D130, D133, D134]
- The lens is the only surface during a conversation; the phone and the website are the surfaces after it. Nothing done on the phone shows on the lens. [ruled: D61]
- The server is where speech-to-text, the wearer/other split, the catch detectors, the joggers and the stores live; the lens app streams audio up and draws what comes down. [chosen]
- What comes down is a view (the dot, no mark, one line, the names page), each timed on the server's clock; the lens keeps no timer of its own but its reconnect, sends its taps up, and decides alone only that with no server Catch cannot hear. [chosen]
- One wearer per account: a pair of glasses delivers to exactly one phone. [ruled: D79]
- The conversation is the wearer's, not the connection's: the server keeps one session per account, keyed by the shared token, so a lens that loses its socket and comes back finds its conversation, its held names and the wearer's own name as it left them; the newest socket counts. A fresh launch of the plugin counts as Catch starting (D129) only when no conversation is open. [chosen]
- Catch hears English only. [ruled: D78]

## The lens, exactly

- Idle: a single dim dot at the lowest brightness, top right. No dot means Catch cannot hear; a dim dash means the wearer paused it. [ruled: D14]
- Gestures, with two meanings each depending on what is showing: tap opens the held page when only the dot shows and clears any line otherwise; long-press ends the conversation now; swipe up asks to keep the conversation when only the dot shows and scrolls back on a page; swipe down pauses or resumes when only the dot shows, declines the keep prompt, turns the names page to promises, and scrolls. Double-tap is Even's exit. [ruled: D34, D40, D43, D86, D92]
- The held page: names in the order heard, scrolling past twenty, then the promises page, the wearer's own first, then those owed; the page dismisses on a tap or after five seconds. [ruled: D43]
- The names page is drawn as three dim text containers (the header `this conversation 1/2`, the names one per line, the hint `↓ promises` at the foot) and the empty page as one; not the SDK's list container, which takes no brightness. [chosen]
- The promises page is two dim text containers: the header `this conversation 2/2`, then the wearer's promises one per line as the lens summarizes them (`send Dev the deck tonight`), then the promises owed to the wearer one per line as `owed: Dev sends the agenda` (P5), or `no promises yet` when there are none either way (D112). A tap with no names held but promises held shows the names page (header and hint, no names) rather than D31's `nothing held`, so the promises can still be reached. A swipe down is the SDK's `SCROLL_BOTTOM_EVENT` on the container that takes the touches (checked only in the simulator's event model, not on real glasses); it turns the names page to the promises page, which then has five seconds of its own, and means nothing yet at the dot (P6 pauses there). [chosen]
- With automatic cut-off detection switched off by the operator, a tap on the bare dot opens the held page with the wearer's last six words as its first line, so the thread can still be asked for. [ruled: D120]
- The lens never shows two things at once: the newest wins; an unanswered keep prompt returns after the line that displaced it. [ruled: D32, D40]
- The thread is the wearer's most recent continuous run of speech, shown as an ellipsis and its last five words (the ellipsis counts as the sixth mark), never reaching back across someone else's turn. [ruled: D48, D131]
- The keep prompt `keep this conversation?` is a dim line in the lower third, where a catch line goes, so a catch line takes its place and gives it back (D42). A swipe up is the SDK's `SCROLL_TOP_EVENT` (checked only in the simulator's event model); it asks only at the bare dot in an open conversation, and with no conversation open there is nothing to keep, so it does nothing; on a line or a page it means nothing yet (P10 scrolls a page back). The prompt has no timer; it goes when a tap keeps (`kept`, a second and a half), when a swipe down declines, or when the conversation it asked about ends. While Catch cannot hear, no prompt shows; it comes back with the dot. [chosen]
- The dropped thread's clause-end signal is the provider's punctuation: the wearer stopped mid-sentence when the last word of their run carries no full stop, question mark or exclamation mark in Deepgram's punctuated transcript (interim results are not used). Someone else has taken the floor at two seconds of continuous speech, the low end of D35's range, where pauses of up to 0.75 s still count as continuous (a tuning value for the shipping step). The line fires once per run, and the run is forgotten whenever Catch stops hearing, since an outage may have swallowed the sentence's end. With detection off (`CATCH_THREAD_DETECTION=off`), the thread is the held page's topmost dim text container, above the header and the names, or above `this conversation · nothing held` when nothing is held. [chosen]
- Glyphs: the dot (•), the middle dot (·), the down arrow (↓), the ellipsis (…) and the dash (– and —) all render in the Even Hub simulator 0.9.5, so Catch uses them as they are, with no ASCII substitute. The check was done in the simulator, not on a real lens (no loaner, D126): every one drew there at full brightness and at the dim level Catch uses (P1a, 2026-10-17, `apps/lens/glyphs.html`). Whether the firmware draws them the same is not known; Even drops missing glyphs silently, so the check is repeated on real glasses if a G2 arrives. [chosen]

## Conversations

- A conversation ends after ten minutes in which the wearer does not speak, whatever anyone else says, or at once on a long-press; at either end the names clear and the promises go to the phone. The first conversation after Catch starts begins on anyone's speech; every later one on the wearer's. A pause freezes the conversation and its timer. [ruled: D92, D129]
- Catch tells the wearer from everyone else first by the Even SDK's per-frame speaker role (self, other, unknown), then by loudness, the mic sitting by the wearer's mouth, and, where neither separates them on the real glasses, by the speech-to-text provider's speaker labels; each signal answers only "the wearer or not". It never knows which other person is speaking; every ruling that names another person rests on a name having been caught. [ruled: D125, awaiting signature; D119 until then]
- Wearer-versus-others is one interface with three implementations: the SDK speaker role (a majority vote of the 100 ms frame roles over each word's time span, with loudness breaking ties and deciding `unknown` frames), near-field energy, and the provider's speaker labels; never voice identification. The speaker role leads if it agrees with the script on at least 80% of the wearer's frames in the hour-one test; otherwise loudness leads. Which one runs is the operator's call, recorded here. The dropped thread's "someone else takes the floor" is continuous other-speech of two to three seconds during the wearer's pause. [chosen]
- A repeated name is a second person only within two minutes; later it is a re-introduction. A name first heard in someone's "meet X" and then in X's own introduction is one person. A timing and wording rule, since identity is out of reach. [ruled: D87, D132]

## The phone and the website

- Three screens and nothing else: a history of conversations newest first, grouped by day, each row with names, time, open count and a kept mark; one screen per conversation with YOU and OWED TO YOU lists, tick boxes, and a link to the jogger; the jogger as one flat list of bullets with the technical terms in bold and the missed-time line where it fell. [ruled: D98, D99, D100]
- The phone receives one notification per conversation that produced promises, written as commands, with a second line for promises owed; a long list is cut off with an ellipsis and opens in full. Promises wait for an unreachable phone however long it takes. [ruled: D28, D38]
- The notification is one per conversation with promises, addressed to the account (the shared token's digest, as the server keys its sessions, D79): its title `Catch`, its body's first line the wearer's promises as commands joined "A, B and C", cut at a word with an ellipsis past 90 characters (about two lines of a phone notification's body, so P5's owed line fits under it; a presentation value, not a ruling), and it carries the conversation's id, which opens that conversation's screen. Each waits in an outbox, in order, tried again every ten seconds on the server's clock until the phone takes it (D114); the outbox keeps every waiting notification in the store too, so a restarted server sends it all the same, once, and a notification leaves the store only when its phone has taken it (P9). There is no phone push yet, so the hosted server has no notifier, and a conversation's promises reach the phone through the phone page's history and its YOU section. [chosen]
- The website is cut (D126): its rows in the screen outlines over a surface column stay scenario-scoped `wip` and are deferred to the fence. [ruled: D103, D115, D126]
- The native app is a preference, protected as a ruling, not a requirement the build must reconstruct. [ruled: D105]
- The phone page is the plugin's own page (`apps/lens/index.html`, `src/phone.ts`), shown by the Even app on the phone beside the glasses: it reads an HTTP API on the server (`GET /phone/history`, `GET /phone/conversations/<id>`, `GET /phone/conversations/<id>/jogger`) with the shared token as a bearer header, on the http(s) twin of the lens's server origin, and makes one write there, a tick (below). The server writes a conversation for the phone only as it ends, and only one that produced a promise or a keep; nothing of any other conversation is ever stored. The open count, on each row and in the header, counts only the wearer's own promises (YOU) not yet done; OWED TO YOU promises are other people's to keep and never count against "all done" (operator answer, P2b′ fix round). [chosen]
- Tick-off is on the phone page (D39, D133; P7), for YOU and OWED TO YOU alike: a tick is `PUT /phone/promises/<id>/done` with the same bearer token, which sets the promise's `done` in the store's file once and never unsets it (there is no untick), so a tick survives a reload and a restart, and answers the conversation's screen as the store now holds it; a promise already ticked answers the same and changes nothing, and a promise of another account's is answered 404 like one that does not exist and is never touched. The page takes a ticked promise off the screen at once and then draws the server's answer; a tick that does not reach the server, or that it refuses, puts the promise back in its place with one quiet line (`Not saved yet. Tick it again in a moment.`), never an error screen; a second tap on a promise whose tick is on its way asks nothing, and a promise the server has confirmed stays off the screen even if an older answer arrives after it. A ticked promise leaves its section; the history's open counts drop for the wearer's own and never move for an owed one. Ticking never touches the lens or `/audio`. [chosen]
- A kept conversation's jogger is written once, as it ends, by one pass of `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` (the promise detector's model since the P4 fix round: `gemini-3.8-flash` on the free tier took 32 to 168 seconds with 503s and twenty requests a day, which cannot meet D116's thirty seconds; the visionary's answer): the model reads the transcript as numbered lines (a sentence, or a run of one side's words, marked Wearer or Other) and the names caught, and answers bullets each tagged with the line it comes from; the code orders them by line, adds the wearer's promises as bullets of their own (`You promised: <summary>`) at the line each was said, and a `N minutes not heard` line where each span Catch could not hear fell (whole minutes, rounded; a span that rounds to none gets no line), and takes the bold off any term the transcript does not contain (looked for one sentence at a time, a term's `#` or `+` kept as part of it, so `C#` is not the letter c and `learning. Rate` is not `learning rate`: risk-3). Nothing caps the count. The jogger is stored with the conversation; the kept notification, `Kept: conversation with <names>` (or `at <time>` in `CATCH_TIME_ZONE` when no name was caught; a zone the server does not know is named in a warning at startup and the server's own is used, risk-2), is queued in the same outbox behind the promise notification once the jogger is stored, and opens the jogger. The conversation screen links to it, `Kept conversation ›`; the jogger screen is headed by the names and the length. Every jogger cassette is the real model's, recorded on `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` in the P4 fix round; its real answers asked the prompt to name what a technical term is (a named tool, product, technique, method or field) and to bold the term alone, not the words around it. [chosen]
- The jogger's tidy pass (P7, the operator's request, no ruling): after the first draft and before the jogger is stored, while the words still live in the pass, a second call on `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` through the same cassette client, grounded on the same numbered transcript, is given the draft and asked to merge bullets that say the same thing or split one point between them, drop filler, fix spoken grammar and keep each bullet one short sentence, in the order things came up, as one flat list with no headings and no fixed count, never more bullets than the draft, adding nothing the transcript does not contain, and copying the code's own bullets (`You promised: …`, `N minutes not heard`) character for character at their places. Its answer gets the honest bold again, then a structural check, and the first draft is stored unchanged if the tidied one loses, doubles, rewords, moves or invents a promise or missed-time line (moved: past another, or among the plain bullets, where a fixed bullet that had plain bullets before it, or after it, in the draft must still have some on that side and a run the draft had empty between fixed bullets must stay empty, merges within a run allowed), loses or reorders a term the draft set in bold, has a heading or an all-bold bullet, has more bullets than the draft or a bullet longer than the draft's longest (and over twenty-five words), says a time of day, or has a capitalized word (a bullet's first word included, a name's possessive read as the name) or a number that neither the transcript nor the draft has. Both passes share D116's thirty seconds on the injected clock, or for a conversation of an hour or more D116's "a few minutes" read as three (the operator's answer, the number the kept-conversations steps use), counted from the conversation's end: a tidy pass that would start after them is not asked, one still out when they run out is cancelled, and one that fails leaves the first draft too; each skip is logged with its reason and never a word of the conversation (a failed pass is also a model error, so a missing cassette fails a lane loudly). The transcript goes once the final jogger is stored (D76). The tidy cassettes are the real model's, recorded with the offline pending flow in P7. [chosen]

## What survives, and what is deleted

- Nothing survives a conversation except the wearer's promises, the promises made to the wearer, and the conversations the wearer marked; an unkept conversation's transcript and audio are deleted everywhere, the server included, at the boundary. [ruled: D76]
- During a conversation the transcript is held so the jogger can be written and so a restart loses nothing: names, promises, the keep mark and the transcript so far survive an app or phone restart. [ruled: D73]
- A restart is taken up on the server, not in the lens: each wearer's session (one per account, D79) writes its state to the store after every change (the held names in order with each name's two-minute window, the wearer's promises and where each was said, the promises owed to the wearer, the keep mark, the spans not heard, when ten minutes of the wearer's silence end it, and where its timeline stands), beside the transcript already held word by word; nothing of what was said is in that state. As the server starts, before it takes a socket, it builds every wearer's session again from the store, so a lens that comes back (the same launch reconnecting, or the Even app started again, D129 still read per plugin launch) re-attaches to the open conversation and never begins a second. From when the last process stopped hearing (the lens gone, the process closing, or for a crash its last save) until a lens is heard again is a span not heard (D53). The quiet rule runs on the server's clock across the restart: a conversation no lens comes back to ends when its ten minutes run out, and one whose ten minutes ran out while no server ran ends as the server starts, at the moment they ran out; either way its transcript is deleted then, like any other (D76), so no abandoned conversation's words outlive it. A promise pass still out, or a kept conversation's jogger still being written, when the process stops is lost. [chosen]
- The open conversation's transcript is held in the store word by word, keyed by the account, from its first word, and deleted at its end, kept or not (a kept conversation's words are read out first and live only in its jogger's model pass until the jogger is stored, then go too: operator note at P4, under D76), with SQLite's `secure_delete` on and the write-ahead log truncated, so the words are gone from the file's bytes too; the session's memory of what was said (the split's word window, the promise detector's context) is cleared at the same moment. No audio is ever stored: the PCM goes to the speech-to-text provider and nowhere else. A restart takes the open conversation up again (P9), so as the server starts it deletes only the transcript rows no open conversation owns (a crash between a word and its session's save, a session row it cannot read). Nothing the server logs about a model pass quotes the prompt or the answer. [chosen]
- For the hackathon the team is trusted as operator and may read what it hosts. [ruled: D77]
- Consent, where the law requires it, is the wearer's job; Catch gives the people the wearer is with no sign they are heard. [ruled: D85]

## Build order and process

- The cut order, earliest built first: staying dark, names, the held page and the conversation boundary with the Even Beta uploaded; the dropped thread; the wearer's own promises and the notification; shipping (the hosted server, the Beta re-upload, thresholds tuned on the real glasses, the demo video, the tester's README, the Devpost entry naming the Even Hub app); keep and the jogger; promises owed to the wearer; pause; restart persistence; scrolling past twenty names (the iPhone app's screens, tick-off on the phone and the website are cut by D126). Anything unbuilt at the last merge is deferred to the fence, not counted as a failure. [ruled: D118, D126]
- The build follows the aBDD workflow: feature files first, `gherkin-node-test` for linting and parsing them, unit tests under the step bindings, and each phase of the phase plan run through the build-pipeline once the plan exists. Every other technology choice (server language, speech-to-text provider, hosting, the model that judges promises and writes joggers) is the build's, within these constraints. [ruled: D106]
- A quick user guide for the touch vocabulary is a deliverable of the build, outside the glasses. [ruled: D72]
- Streaming speech-to-text with word timings, chosen for the best handling of names on the team's own names Saturday morning; a name line within two seconds, a promise line within three. [chosen]
- Speech-to-text is Deepgram (D126): streaming `nova-3` with word timings, interim results and diarization, opted out of model-improvement retention (`mip_opt_out`), behind one interface (`server/src/stt/`) so another provider is one adapter; the key is a Fly secret and never ships in the lens. [chosen]
- Deepgram's stream is opened with `endpointing=300` and `utterance_end_ms=1000` (PT), because without them, on the simulator, finals came in late batches during continuous speech and catches lagged further as a conversation ran on: finals now come at a pause of 300 ms, and Deepgram's `UtteranceEnd` (a second with no words) ends the sentence being gathered, so a promise is judged at a natural pause and not only at the provider's punctuation; a final Deepgram marks `speech_final` (its endpointing's pause) ends it there too, at that final's last word, and a sentence already ended is not ended again, so the `UtteranceEnd` that may follow judges no promise twice (operator answer 4, PT fix round). Names are caught from interim results too, so a name said mid-turn shows within D17's two seconds while its final lags: the interim words are split on the frames like any word, D29, `CATCH_WEARER_NAME`, D87, D88 and D132 apply, and the name is held tentatively; the final words confirm it with no second line and no `(2)`, and a name they do not give once a batch of them reaches past the introduction (or its utterance ends, or the stream is lost) is withdrawn from the held names and from a names page showing, its line taken down at once if still up (operator answer 1). A batch reaches past it only when it does so both by time (its last word ends past the middle of the introduction's last word in the interim) and by count (the provider has sent as many final words as came before that word, and the word itself): final timings that come half a word late can put a batch cut just before the name over the name's interim time, and withdrawing then would take the name down and show it again from the next batch (PT fix round, risk-1); a batch that passes only one way leaves the decision to the next batch, the utterance's end or the stream's. A name caught from interim words that would open the conversation (D129) is caught tentatively like any other and withdrawn if the finals do not open it (operator answer 3). A tentative name is never stored for a restart, never given to the promise detector and never among the names a conversation ends with. Promises, the thread and the transcript stay on the finals. The fixture provider can answer the same way (`test/harness/provider-model.ts`: interims every 300 ms, finals at pauses past the endpointing or after about 4 s of continuous speech, utterance ends), and the latency test asserts D17 and D89 on it, on fixture time. [chosen]
- The split in use is loudness, a word at or above a near-field threshold being the wearer's (default -20 dBFS, a tuning value for the shipping step, set by `CATCH_LOUDNESS_THRESHOLD_DBFS`), because off the glasses the SDK's speaker role is always unknown (D126). The speaker-role vote and the provider's labels are built behind the same interface (`CATCH_SPLIT`) and run on both fixture variants; the provider's labels are mapped to the wearer by the speaker-role vote, which falls back to loudness. `npm run local` sets the threshold to -25 dBFS when the `.env` leaves it unset, because on a laptop microphone (a MacBook Air) -20 put the wearer's normal voice under it and -25 told the wearer at about 30 cm from another voice at about 2 m (operator, P5); the server's own default and the fixtures stay at -20. [chosen]
- The promise detector's candidates are the wearer's sentences (ended by the provider's punctuation, a change of speaker, or the provider's `UtteranceEnd` at a pause, PT) that carry a commitment cue (`I'll`, `I will`, `I'm going to`, `let me`, `I promise`, `will do`); every other sentence costs no model pass. Each pass is given the names caught and the four sentences before it, and answers JSON, a promise and its summary or no promise; a conversation's end waits for the passes still out, each at most until D89's three seconds on the clock; a pass that answers later shows no line but its promise is still held (operator answer 9). Without `GEMINI_API_KEY` the server runs with no detector and promises nothing. The P2a cassettes, first written by hand with no key at hand, were re-recorded from the real model in P4; the real answers showed the prompt naming the last person caught rather than the one the wearer had just addressed, so the prompt now says the name the wearer last addressed, in this sentence or their own sentence before it, in the wearer's own word order, keeping every other word the wearer said, possessives and articles included (`send Ana my transcript by Friday`, never `send Ana transcript by Friday`: P4 fix round, operator answer 4). [chosen]
- Promises owed to the wearer (P5): another person's sentence with the same commitment cue gets a pass of its own prompt, which answers the action alone, with no subject and no name in it, leaving out the "you" that names the wearer as the one receiving (`send the agenda`, never `send Dev the agenda`); the three seconds, the end's wait and the late answer are the wearer's. Who promised is never the model's guess (the fence's "Speaker identification"): it is the last name caught in the conversation when the sentence ends, or, before any name has been caught, an unnamed promiser, numbered `they (1)`, `they (2)` in the order heard among those held unnamed; a name caught later attaches to the most recent promiser (a pass still out counts, one that said no promise does not) if that one is unnamed, and to nobody otherwise (D113). The lens shows `Dev promised · send the agenda` or `they promised · …`; the held line, the notification's second line (`Owed to you: Dev sends the agenda`, cut on its own like the first) and the phone page's OWED TO YOU put the verb in the third person behind the promiser, by rule, not by the model. A conversation with owed promises and none of the wearer's still notifies, with the owed line alone. [chosen]
- One model pass per catch for promises (judge, summarize, keep the time word, fill the object from the other person's last sentence) and one per kept conversation for the jogger; names by pattern on the transcript. The model is Gemini Flash through the Gemini API: `gemini-3.5-flash-lite` for the promise pass and, since the P4 fix round, for the jogger too (the visionary's answer: `gemini-3.8-flash` could not meet D116's thirty seconds on the free tier); both ids pinned, never a retiring or preview id. The key is on the Gemini free tier (operator, 2026-10-17): free-tier content may be used by Google to improve its models, disclosed in the README. [chosen]
- The demo day is captured live. The loud-venue backup, in order: a quieter corner; then, as the last resort, a committed fixture played through the real pipeline to the real glasses, said out loud to the judge as a recording and never passed off as live. [chosen]
- The server is TypeScript on Node with SQLite as its one-file store, so lens, server, website and tests share one language, one typecheck and one set of types. It runs on Fly.io from the first phase (app `catch-g2`, region `lax`, a volume for the store), reached at one `wss://` origin whitelisted in `app.json`, because the Even brief requires a README a tester can follow after the laptop is closed, and because a WebView served over HTTPS may refuse a plain LAN WebSocket; the laptop is optional for iteration. [chosen]
- The server runs on the build machine as a first-class path: one command starts it on the loopback with the lens in the simulator pointed at it; the hosted copy on Fly.io serves the Beta and testers and is never required by a test, a gate or the demo. [ruled: D128]
- The server runs on Node 24 straight from its TypeScript sources (Node strips the types; no build step), with Node's built-in `node:sqlite` as the store driver, so the container holds one runtime dependency (`ws`). The lens-to-server audio frame is a 16-byte header (sequence number, capture time, `speakerRole`, raw `direction`) before the PCM, defined once in `shared/wire.ts`. [chosen]
- No live model call in any test: detectors and the jogger writer go through one client with record-and-replay cassettes committed beside the fixtures; a missing cassette fails loudly; re-recording is by hand behind an environment flag, or offline for a slow model: a lane run under `CATCH_LLM_PENDING=1` lists the prompts it found missing, and `test/cassettes/rerecord.ts --pending` records them. [chosen]
- Two test lanes: a fast lane (oxlint, one incremental typecheck, unit tests, the current phase's feature files) for every agent inside a phase, and a full lane (everything) for the gate alone. The suite is never type-checked twice. [chosen]
- Tests never call the real speech-to-text provider: fixtures are pre-transcribed word timings on a virtual clock, with timing bounds asserted on fixture time; the real provider runs only by hand behind an environment flag. The Even bridge stub is in-process and one server serves each test worker. [chosen]
